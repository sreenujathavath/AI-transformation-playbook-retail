AI strategy: where i-Q Retail stands and what we do in the next 90 days

By Sreenu Jathavath, Sr HR / AI Strategist / Consultant. Board briefing for i-Q Retail. Figures marked "assumption" are planning hypotheses to validate.

The case

i-Q Retail is a mid-size specialty retailer (apparel, home goods, lifestyle accessories) with 200 stores, an e-commerce site, a mobile app and 12M loyalty members. It has no AI in production; some teams use free public tools informally, and vendors have pitched "AI-powered" personalization, but nothing has been bought.

The trigger: a national competitor launched an AI personal shopper, and the board asked the CEO for the company's AI strategy.

The task: a senior manager on the digital strategy team has two weeks to brief the board on what AI is, what the company can realistically do with it, and what the next 90 days should hold.

Recommended answer to the board

We should respond with a focused, governed program, not a headline purchase. Over 90 days we fix the basics (policy, data, ownership), pilot four use cases that touch customers and operations, and return to the board with measured results and a costed scale-up decision.

Our edge is not the technology, which any retailer can rent. It is 12M loyalty members' purchase history across 200 stores and digital channels, used responsibly.

Why now

A visible competitor move changes customer expectations and board attention. Informal staff use of public tools is already a data risk, so doing nothing is not neutral.

Why not a big bet

No data audit, no owner and no policy exist yet. We have not seen the competitor's results, only its launch. A fast follower with proof beats a copycat with a bill.

Path to the board date

Week 1: executive interviews, data and tool inventory, vendor claims review. Week 2: draft, CDO review, CEO preview, finalise.

AI in plain terms

AI is software that finds patterns in data and uses them to predict, create or act. It is not a single product. It is a set of capabilities we combine with our own data and processes, and its value depends on those inputs.

Predict

Predictive AI

Learns from history to forecast what happens next.

Retail use: demand forecasts, size and returns prediction, next-best offer.

Maturity: proven. Risk: moderate, needs clean data.

Create

Generative AI

Produces text, images and answers from prompts and context.

Retail use: product descriptions, service replies, associate help, campaign copy.

Maturity: fast-moving. Risk: errors and brand tone, needs human review.

Act

Agentic AI

Chains steps and uses tools to complete a task toward a goal.

Retail use: the competitor's personal shopper: builds outfits, checks stock, answers follow-ups.

Maturity: early. Risk: highest, needs strong guardrails.

What AI is not

  • Not a product we can buy and switch on. "AI-powered" vendor claims need testing against our data.
  • Not accurate by default. Generative tools can state wrong facts confidently.
  • Not only a technology project. Process, policy and training decide the outcome.

What the competitor's launch tells us

  • Customers will expect recommendations tied to their own history.
  • It combines three things: a good product catalog, customer history, and a generative model. We hold two of the three in some form; catalog quality needs checking.
  • It shows a feature, not proven sales or margin impact.

How the dots connect

  • Marketing + Merchandising: product content and recommendations feed the same catalog, so fix catalog data once.
  • Loyalty data: powers recommendations, offers and forecasting, so consent and privacy rules come first.
  • Stores + e-commerce: one inventory view lets a shopper experience suggest items that are actually in stock.
  • Service + stores: an internal assistant tested with staff builds the skills and trust needed before customer-facing agents.

The next 90 days

Three phases, each ending in a gate where the steering group decides to continue, adjust or stop. Nothing scales without evidence.

Days 1–30: align and baseline

  • Name an executive owner and a cross-functional steering group (Digital, Marketing, Merch, Ops, IT, Legal, Privacy, HR).
  • Issue interim AI acceptable-use policy; move staff to approved tools.
  • Inventory current AI use, data sources and vendor claims.
  • Score and pick four pilots; set baseline metrics.
Gate 1: policy live, pilots chosen, owners and success measures agreed.

Days 31–60: build and test

  • Run two internal pilots (e.g. product content, agent-assist) with human review.
  • Prototype one recommendation pilot on a loyalty segment with consent checks.
  • Test two or three vendors on our data against the build option.
  • Train pilot users; log errors and feedback.
Gate 2: pilots live with no unresolved privacy or brand issues.

Days 61–90: prove and decide

  • Run controlled tests against baseline.
  • Cost the scale-up and the personal shopper option, including run costs.
  • Draft the 12-month roadmap and operating model.
  • Report to the board.
Gate 3: board decides: scale, adjust or stop, based on results.

Decisions we ask of the board

  • Endorse a time-boxed 90-day program with a pilot budget to be sized with Finance (assumption: modest, not a platform commitment).
  • Confirm an executive owner and steering group.
  • Endorse guardrails: customer data consent, human review, no sensitive data in public tools.
  • Agree to review results and a scale decision at day 90.
